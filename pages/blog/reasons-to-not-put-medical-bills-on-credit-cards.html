When faced with medical debt, it can be tempting to resort to credit cards as a solution. However, relying on credit cards to pay off medical expenses can have long-lasting negative consequences for your financial future. In this blog post, we will look into three important reasons why using credit cards for medical bills should be avoided.

Three Reasons You Shouldn’t Use Credit Cards for Medical Bills

#1. Loss of Negotiating Power:

One crucial reason to avoid putting medical debt on a credit line is the loss of negotiating power. By moving your debt away from the medical provider and onto a credit card or a home equity line of credit (HELOC), you relinquish the ability to dispute charges or negotiate payment terms directly with the provider. This can result in a loss of control over your debt and limit your ability to find a resolution that suits your financial situation.

When you deal directly with the medical provider, you have the opportunity to negotiate payment plans or even request financial assistance based on your circumstances. (Read our three steps to deal with any medical debt!) This negotiation process can result in more favorable terms and potentially reduced debt amounts. By transferring the debt to a credit card, you forgo these negotiation opportunities and the ability to find a mutually agreeable solution.

#2. Absence of Zero-Interest Payment Plans:

Another significant disadvantage of paying medical bills with credit cards is the absence of zero-interest payment plans. When you negotiate directly with your medical provider, you often have the opportunity to arrange a zero-interest payment plan. This type of plan allows you to pay off your debt over a specified period without incurring any additional interest charges.

However, if you opt for credit cards or lines of credit to cover medical expenses, you are unlikely to secure a zero-interest payment plan. Credit cards typically carry high-interest rates, making it challenging to pay off the debt promptly. The accumulation of interest charges can exacerbate your financial burden, making it harder to overcome the debt in a timely manner.

Even if you find a credit card that promises up to three years of no interest–life happens! You never know what’s going to happen in that year, and risking your financial future at a high-interest rate is dangerous.

#3. Loss of Credit Score Protections:

Using credit cards to pay off medical debt can result in the loss of certain credit score protections specifically afforded to medical debt. Medical debt is treated differently by credit agencies, recognizing the unique nature of healthcare expenses and providing more leniency when assessing its impact on your credit score and your credit report. For example, unlike consumer debt, medical debt won’t appear on your credit report if less than a year old, paid off, or under $500.

Transferring medical debt to a credit card removes these special protections, subjecting your credit score to potential negative consequences. Credit cards, even “medical credit cards” or “care cards”, are reported to credit agencies as regular consumer debt, and any missed or late payments can have a detrimental effect on your credit score. This can hinder your ability to secure future loans, obtain favorable interest rates, or even impact your job opportunities and housing options.

Takeaways

While it may be tempting to rely on credit cards to manage medical debt, it is important to understand the potential drawbacks and consider alternative options. By putting medical debt on credit cards, you forfeit your negotiating power with medical providers, miss out on zero-interest payment plans, and lose the credit score protections specific to medical debt.

Instead of relying on credit cards, explore other avenues for managing medical debt. Engage in direct negotiation with your medical provider–discuss payment plans or financial assistance programs that may be available. Finally, always follow our three steps to crushing your medical bills.

Prioritizing your financial planning and seeking assistance from financial advisors or credit counseling services can provide valuable guidance and help you navigate the complexities of managing medical debt effectively. By making informed decisions and exploring alternative solutions, you can safeguard your financial future, avoid unnecessary debt accumulation, and protect your credit score.

Remember, the impact of medical debt extends beyond immediate financial strain. It can have long-lasting repercussions on your overall financial well-being. Take proactive steps to address medical debt responsibly, seeking out options that align with your long-term goals and prioritizing your financial stability.
