Get rid of medical bills in 3 simple steps

Medical debt can be crippling. We know that from personal experience and from our experience as professionals in the medical field. We’ve seen it from the side of the provider and the side of the patient.

And we want to let you know that medical debt is broken. It simply doesn’t work. It’s unfair, it’s unethical, and it can destroy your finances.

Fortunately, we have developed a checklist to help you navigate medical debt, know how to pay hospital bills, get the best price on procedures, and pay only the fair amount.

Look, we all know that doctors are experts and that they can be lifesavers. They deserve to be paid. But the healthcare system is broken, and most of what you’re paying in medical bills does not go into the doctors’ pockets. It goes to feed a system that exploits the most critically sick and injured at their moment of weakness.

If that’s you, you need to know about the medical bill checklist.

Why You Should Always Check Your Medical Bill

Your medical bills, when you receive them, very often have elevated costs. They present you with a high number and expect you to pay it. They will get you on a payment plan with a high interest rate–and that’s if the debt collectors don’t come calling.

Many people resort to medical credit cards, personal loans, and even second mortgages to pay their medical expenses. Don’t do it!

We’ll explain more in the downloadable medical bill checklist, but you should always ask for an itemized bill with CPT codes.

Why You Need The Medical Bill Checklist

The Medical Bill Checklist is the clearest and most concise document describing the realities of medical billing and the negative impact that medical debt can have on your credit score and credit report.

We outline three basic steps–nothing complicated, but extremely important–that will revolutionize the way you pay your medical bills.

Best of all, the Medical Bill Checklist is completely free. All we need is an email address and your name, and the entire document is yours.

Knowledge is power. And if you’ve got medical debt collectors hounding you, it’s time to get knowledge on your side.
